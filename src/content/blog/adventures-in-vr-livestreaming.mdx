  The tutorial was easy enough to follow, but there were a few problems, perhaps
  owing to the video being 2 years old, or perhaps software updates.
</p>
<p>
  One, the OBS workflow requiring a TIFF had some particularities about how the
  EXR was converted into a TIFF. Opening in Photoshop asked whether you wanted
  alpha as transparency or an alpha channel: I found that “alpha
  channel” worked.

<p>
  These were the TIFF settings I used. I played around a lot, but was not able
  to make a definitively exhaustive finding of which settings mattered. It might
  have all just been that first alpha channel setting. While his workflow
  recommended GIMP,{" "}
  <em>
    I found that GIMP was not causing a color shift and not exporting the TIFF
    STMap correctly.
  </em>
</p>
<p>
  Hugh says you have to use the modded OBS that’s present in his links; I’m not
  entirely sure what’s so bespoke about his version, because it seemed to me
  that he had just included this github plug-in for OBS:{" "}
  <a href="https://github.com/exeldro/obs-shaderfilter">
    https://github.com/exeldro/obs-shaderfilter
  </a>
</p>
<p>
  Perhaps due to an implementation of that plug-in, here were other oddities I
  noticed. Neither EXR nor TIFF are explicitly allowed in the filepicker; you
  have to set to All Files. The shader accepts EXR, but appears to quantize it
  to a lower bit-depth, the preview footage was HEAVILY pixellated. Probably
  interpreting the EXR as 16 or even 8 bit.
</p>
<p>
  However, even with the TIFF, we noticed on-set some stairstepping, aliasing,
  jaggedness. Perhaps a resolution limitation. Hard to diagnose whether that,
  too, was an implementation quirk of the OBS shaderfilter.
</p>
<p>
  Overall, if you can afford Touchdesigner (or can bum a key off someone) it
  appears to be the more robust solution. Touchdesigner could take the EXR
  directly (no transforming to TIFF), and did not seem as aliased as the OBS
  solution.
</p>
<p>
  Central Control worked fine, and overall the latency wasn’t terrible, 1–2
  seconds. Sometimes it would hang and there would be dropouts, but that seemed
  to be an inevitability for the hardware.
</p>
<p>
  I tried for a while to see if I could vibecode an alternative to Central
  Control that would expose the bitrate controls, because I wondered if I could
  improve the quality, but it may be that the final results were simply limited
  by 4k capture, not bitrate. In an ideal world I could figure out how to get a
  higher-res stream like 8k streaming into the quest, but I may need to do more
  robust research for that.
</p>
